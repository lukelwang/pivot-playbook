30 Minute Pivot Playbook

Strong stocks pull back to the 21 EMA, shake out intraday, and we buy the break of the first strong green bar. The stop is fixed. Sell half once the close is 3 ATR above the 21 EMA, and trail the rest with the 10 EMA. "Rules explained" uses constructed textbook bars to show each case clearly. "Model book" shows real historical trades so you can see how the rules look in real price action.

Daily checklist

The whole strategy boils down to what you do at three points in the day. The charts below explain each item.

Before the open

  1. Universe is leaders only, RS top 5%. Close > 50 day MA > 200 day MA, 200 day MA rising, within 10% of the 52 week high.
  2. Keep only names whose prior close is no more than 0.7 daily ATR above the 21 EMA.
  3. Note four numbers for each name. The prior day low, the weekly 8 EMA, ADR20 (average daily range over the last 20 days, in percent), and the daily ATR (used for pullback depth, also shown in the indicator tooltip).
  4. Check the account. How much room is left under the 3% total open risk cap, how much buying power is left, and whether the account is down 8% from its peak (if so, halve all risk).

During the session (30 minute chart)

  1. Wait for 3 or more red bars in a row. They can start anywhere, including overnight.
  2. The first green bar that closes in its upper third is the pivot. If it closes in the middle, wait up to 2 more bars.
  3. Size by pullback depth, measured from the high of the first red bar to the lowest red bar low, in daily ATR. At 0.7 or more risk 0.2% per trade (indicator shows P+). From 0.4 to 0.7 risk 0.1% (P). Under 0.4, skip it.
  4. Place a buy stop at the pivot high + 1 tick, with the stop at the pattern low minus 1 tick. Shares = risk amount ÷ risk per share.
  5. If the stop distance (as a percent of price) is 60% of ADR20 or more, pass on the pivot.
  6. Cancel the order if price breaks the pattern low before a fill. The order stays live until the next day's close (if it fills on a gap at the next open, recompute shares at the actual fill).
  7. When there is no room (3% total risk, or not enough buying power), deep pullbacks (0.2%) come first, and within the same tier the higher RS name wins. At 0.2% per trade the 3% cap is rarely hit; in a cash account buying power runs out first.

After the close

  1. The stop order stays in place and never moves.
  2. From the day after entry, if the close is more than 3 ATR above the 21 EMA, sell half at the next open (only once).
  3. If the close is below the 10 EMA, sell the rest at the next open (do not sell early at that close).
  4. Log it. Distance from the 21 EMA in ATR, pullback depth, any bonus factors, whether the pivot came right away, whether it was 3 red bars from the open, and the final R.

TradingView indicator Pivot30

The entry rules are built into a Pine v6 indicator (~/code/pivot30.pine, v2.9, passed review by both the advisor and Codex). It runs only on a standard 30 minute chart and targets US stocks (using the SPY trading calendar). The indicator handles entry signals only. Strict mode is the default. All of the prior day's daily conditions (price, dollar volume, trend, 52 week high, distance from the 21 EMA) must pass before it draws a P or places an order. Each condition can be unchecked in the settings to loosen the filter. Screen for RS top 5% yourself (TradingView has no market wide RS ranking).

How to use it

  • Hover over a P to see the number of red bars, trigger, stop, 1R as a percent of price and of ADR20, distance from the 21 EMA in ATR, pullback depth and suggested size, bonus factors (for reference only), and whether the daily conditions PASS or FAIL that day.
  • The indicator tooltip still suggests the sizes used before Sep 29 (0.4% and 0.2%). Until the indicator is updated, this page decides: deep 0.2%, medium 0.1%.
  • The "Size by pullback depth" setting is on by default. Turn it off to go back to the old bonus tiers, where P+ means a bonus factor is present (0.4%) and P means none (0.1%).
  • Pivots with a stop at 60% of ADR20 or more are hidden by default (you can turn that off to show them as a gray P wide).
  • The moving average filter (off by default) lets you check 21EMA, weekly 8EMA, 8EMA and 5EMA. Only pivots whose pattern low tags a checked average (within 0.25 ATR or below it) are shown. Checking several works as "or", so more checks means looser. In the backtest only 21EMA alone made a difference, +0.32R per trade when tagged versus negative 0.37R when not (found after the fact, pending holdout data).
  • The table shows today's daily conditions, with strict or report only in the title. Unchecked conditions show off. To see whether an older P passed at the time, read its tooltip.
  • Be careful with exceptions. To loosen a rule, uncheck it, but avoid loosening "too far from the 21 EMA". Those pivots have historically done about as well as buying at random. Turning off strict mode goes back to price pattern only, with the daily conditions just reported.
  • Fills and cancels use 1 minute data, the same as the backtest. Within the same minute the fill counts first. Fill price is the higher of the trigger and that minute's open, plus 1 tick of slippage. The stop is at least 0.1 daily ATR from the trigger.
  • Each event shows a single text marker and all history is kept. Markers on roughly the last 2000 bars have hover tooltips (trigger, stop, bonus factors, daily conditions).
  • The indicator does not know your positions and shows every P. If you already hold the stock, the rules say do not add.

Step 1. Daily location

At the close before entry, price can be no more than 0.7 daily ATR above the 21 EMA. This is the only condition that made pivots beat random entries in all three data periods. The universe itself (RS top 5% leaders, moving averages stacked up, within 10% of the 52 week high) is a given.

Step 2. Entry on the 30 minute chart

Three or more red bars in a row, followed by the first green bar that closes in the upper third of its range, make a pivot. The red bars can start anywhere, at the open, midday, or carried over from the prior afternoon. Place a buy stop at the pivot high + 1 tick, good until the next day's close. Cancel if the pattern low breaks before a fill. The stop goes at the pattern low minus 1 tick. Pass if the stop distance is 60% of ADR20 or more. Those pivots have historically made about 0R per trade, and negative 0.84R among leaders.

Does the shape of the red bars matter? Only depth does

Depth is the drop from the high of the first red bar to the lowest low of all the red bars (excluding wait bars and the pivot bar), measured in daily ATR. For example, with a daily ATR of 2 dollars, a first red bar high of 100 and a red bar low of 98.40, depth is 0.8, a deep pullback. It can be computed at the close of the P bar. Sample is 21 EMA pullbacks with stops under 60% ADR, using this strategy's exits, recomputed on Sep 29 on the current sample with overlapping pivots of one stock counted separately (so the counts are higher than in the statistics tab).

No stable difference showed up for whether each red bar is lower than the last, how much red bars overlap, bar count, growing or shrinking bodies, or whether the moving average slope is up or down. The 0.4 and 0.7 cutoffs were chosen after seeing the data and await the holdout test (ADR 0006 H7).

Any red bar counts

The code checks one thing only. A close below the open is a red bar. Lower highs or lower lows, body size, long lower wicks, none of that matters. Every chart below was checked with the strategy's detection code and triggers a pivot. These charts are about shape only. Whether to take the trade also depends on pullback depth, which uses daily ATR and appears in the indicator tooltip.

These do not count

Four cases are not traded. The first three void the pattern. A doji or green bar before 3 red bars are counted resets the count. A new red bar while waiting for the pivot voids the old pattern and starts a new count at 1. Three bars in a row that are not qualifying green bars after the count is complete make the pattern expire. In the fourth case the pattern is valid but the stop is too wide, so we pass.

Step 3. Selling after entry

The stop stays put. From the second trading day after entry, check two things at each close. If the close is more than 3 ATR above the 21 EMA, sell half at the next open (only once). If the close is below the 10 EMA, sell everything left at the next open. If the stop is hit intraday, exit at the stop price. On a gap down, exit at the open.

Step 4. Position size

On a deep pullback a 1R loss equals 0.2% of the account, on a medium pullback 0.1%, and shallow pullbacks are skipped (cut on Sep 29 from 0.4% and 0.2%). Total open risk stays at or under 3%. After an 8% drawdown from the account peak, all risk per trade is halved until a new high. The 0.2% comes from a stated rule: allowing for the error in the expectancy itself, the chance of a maximum drawdown above 18% must be at most 10%, and the base risk is the largest value that meets it (table below).

Working out the share count

With a tight stop the computed position value can get very large. In practice you can cap the position value per trade, say at 25% of the account, and buy at the cap when the calculation exceeds it. Actual risk is then below the planned 0.2% or 0.1%. At 0.2% per trade the median deep pullback position is about 15% of the account. This is a practical tip and is not part of the backtest.

How the base risk was set

Rule: the chance of a maximum drawdown above 18% is at most 10%. Each run first resamples the leader trades in whole calendar months to draw an expectancy (median +0.65R, 10th percentile +0.23R), then simulates a full market account at that expectancy: 2x margin, 3% cap on total open risk, risk halved after an 8% drawdown, leaders only, every fill taken in time order while there is room (about 430 a year), positions marked to market daily. 1,000 runs per base risk.

The largest base risk that meets the rule is 0.2%. Up to 0.3% the bad case (10th percentile growth) still improves a little (10% to 13%), but the chance of a drawdown above 18% rises to about 20%; above 0.3% the bad case gets worse and only the drawdown grows. 18% is the drawdown tolerance this playbook uses; with your own number, read your row off the table. Limits: the rules were picked after seeing the data, and that bias is not in the resampling, so 0.2% is an upper bound; every run shares one 2.5 year market history; margin interest is not charged (about 0.5 to 1 point a year). In a cash account (no leverage), at the in sample expectancy, 0.2% gives about 36% median yearly growth, and about 21% of fills do not fit for lack of buying power. After the holdout test H1 reports, the same rule is rerun.

Discipline

  • When there is room, take every signal that fits the rules. Do not pick. Profits come from a few big winners, and missing one can wipe out a year.
  • When there is no room, follow the priority. Deep pullbacks first, and within the same tier the higher RS name. With leaders only there are about 450 fills a year market wide; at 0.2% per trade the 3% risk cap is rarely hit, and in a cash account buying power runs out first.
  • Leaders only (RS top 5%). Leaders made about 0.53R more per trade than other names (90% interval 0.00 to +1.13), but not in every period: in the first development half the other names did better. Most of the difference is in the big winners, so leader signals are the ones you can least afford to miss. Days with no position are normal. Do not trade names outside the RS top 5% just to have something to do.
  • At 150 live trades, if the average is below negative 0.3R per trade, stop and review.
  • Do not trade 1 or 2 red bar pivots, fixed price targets, a 21 EMA trailing stop, order flow bubbles, or market index on/off switches.
  • Do not switch to selling at the close of the day it breaks the 10 EMA, and do not swap in the 10 day SMA. Both did worse than the current rule (about 0.05R and 0.03R less per trade). After a strong stock closes below the 10 EMA, the next open is on average a better sale than the prior close.
  • Do not "buy the reclaim". Buying at the close right after price reclaims the prior day low or VWAP (or placing a buy stop at that bar's high) makes about 0R per trade. With the same prior day low sweep, waiting for a pivot to form and then buying made +0.33R, and +0.75R among leaders. The prior day low is a bonus factor, not an entry signal.
  • Skip shallow pullbacks (under 0.4 ATR), however clean the pattern looks. The tier's expectancy is about 0R, with the big winners and a pile of 1R losses canceling out.
  • Pass when the stop is 60% of ADR20 or more. Do not force it because the pattern looks good. A wide stop does not lose more money (you buy fewer shares), but the trade has to run a long way to reach 3R, and the expectancy is poor.
  • Leave the order live until the next day's close. Do not cancel it the same day. We tested it, and total R drops by 30% to 40%.
  • The red, green, inside bar pattern is for observation only and not traded, pending the forward test.
  • Pivots after 3 or more red bars straight from the open are traded as normal. Tag them separately in your trade log to build your own sample.

How much to trust these numbers

The entry pattern, the location filter, the exit, the leaders cut and the depth sizing were all chosen after looking at this sample, so every figure here is optimistic. Two tests decide whether the edge is real. The cross section holdout (ADR 0006) uses 4,696 symbol months never used in research; its data finishes downloading around Oct 3, 2026, and each test runs once. The forward test (ADR 0005) reports around April 2027.

Model book, 100 winning pivots

Every trade comes from real 1 minute data from Mar 2024 to Sep 2026, and each one meets all the current rules (the 54 trades from names that are not leaders fail only the RS rule). RS top 5%, prior close no more than 0.7 ATR above the 21 EMA, a break of the first strong green bar after 3 or more red 30 minute bars, stop under 60% ADR, and pullback depth of at least 0.4 daily ATR. The top chart is the 30 minute entry and the bottom chart is the daily exit. R is the simulated result under the rules (half trimmed plus the remaining half), not the best exit picked in hindsight.

How these were picked. These 100 were picked on Sep 27 from the sample of that day (old 10 session hold, 131 leader trades); the statistics tab is current, and the sizes on the charts follow the Sep 29 rule (0.2% and 0.1%). At the time there were 131 leader trades that fit the rules, and only 46 of them made money. All 46 come first, sorted by R from high to low. 26 made 2R or more, 9 made 1R to 2R, and 11 made less than 1R. To reach 100, the other 54 come from RS top 20% names that are not leaders, with every other rule the same, taking the 54 best (all above +4.2R). Under the current rules these names are not traded and are here only to show more patterns. Under the new rules the big shallow pullback winners (CEG +20.9R, STX +15.7R, CAMT +11.9R, ESLT, TER, and two INSM trades) are not here because they are skipped. Looking only at winners makes the win rate look better than it is. About 65% of leader trades that fit the rules lose money, most of them about 1R. The 54 trades from names that are not leaders are the best picked out of 936, so they are even less typical.
